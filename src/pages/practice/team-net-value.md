whatIs: >-
  The [Team Net Value (TNV)](https://www.peakteams.io/methodology#tnv) is a
  single score from 0 to 100 that shows how much of a team's potential value is
  actually realised. It treats the team like a small business with its own
  profit & loss statement and builds on the Input-Mediator-Output-Input (IMOI)
  model from team science. The calculation has been first introduced by research
  of the [Peakteams](https://www.peakteams.io). 


  Team members answer an anonymous survey covering 18 KPIs in four groups:


  * **Input** (5 KPIs): purpose & objectives, values & vision, environment & resources, team organization, leadership principles

  * **Process** (10 KPIs): social cohesion, innovation & growth, delivery methodology, inner drive, identification & engagement, feedback & development, motivation & focus, trust & safety, communication culture, conflict navigation

  * **Output** (2 KPIs): team output, organizational impact

  * **Friction** (1 KPI): energy lost to bureaucracy, politics, rework and unproductive meetings (higher is worse)


  Input, Process and Output create value; Friction acts as a tax on that value:


  **TNV = Value(Input, Process, Output) x (1 - Friction Tax)**


  The friction tax is squared, so mild friction costs little while severe friction wipes out most of the value a team creates. A happy team can still create nothing - TNV measures value, not mood.
whyDo: >-
  Most team surveys measure happiness. But a happy team can deliver nothing, and
  a team that hits every target can still burn out its people. Team Net Value
  measures net value creation instead of sentiment.


  - **One comparable number** for every team, on the same 0-100 scale, so teams and runs can be compared over time

  - **Find the root cause**: shows whether problems come from inputs (direction, resources, leadership) or from execution inside the team

  - **Make friction visible**: bureaucracy, politics and rework are shown as a tax on the value the team creates

  - **Don't trust averages**: every KPI is read together with the spread of answers (Consensus Matrix), so disagreement cannot hide behind a decent mean

  - **Focus on one thing**: instead of a long list of fixes, the team gets one high-leverage next step

  - **Show progress**: re-measuring after about 90 days shows whether the interventions worked
howTo: >-
  1. **Set up the team**: name the team, add the members and share a personal,
  anonymous survey link with everyone.

  2. **Run the survey**: each member answers around 57 questions on a 1-5 scale (about 12 minutes). Wait until at least five members have answered so no individual answers can be identified.

  3. **Score the KPIs**: each of the 18 KPIs is the mean of its questions. Then calculate Input, Process, Output and Friction.

  4. **Calculate Team Net Value**: Value = (mean(Input, Process, Output) - 1) / 4 x 100. Friction tax = ((Friction - 1) / 4)^2. TNV = Value x (1 - Friction tax). Example: friction at 3.0 costs a quarter of the value, at 4.0 more than half.

  5. **Interpret the score**: below 40 needs attention, 40-60 is average, above 60 is a high-performing team.

  6. **Check agreement**: plot every KPI by average score and spread of answers in a Consensus Matrix. High score and high agreement = true strength. High score but low agreement = hidden concern ("silent killer"). Low score and high agreement = known challenge. Low score and low agreement = unclear, build a shared picture first.

  7. **Pick One Big Thing**: check in a fixed order and stop at the first trigger: psychological safety (stop the bleeding), goal clarity (reset the compass), friction/burnout (cut the fat), hidden disagreement (bridge the gap), otherwise push for impact. Turn it into three concrete actions, each with an owner and a timeframe.

  8. **Debrief and re-measure**: discuss the results with the whole team, work on the One Big Thing and repeat the survey after about 90 days to compare the two runs.
